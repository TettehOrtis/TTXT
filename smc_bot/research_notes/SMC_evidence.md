# SMC / ICT evidence review and hypotheses for BT6/BT9+

Date: 2026-09-29. Scope: web research + reading BT1-BT5, BT7 reports. No trading, no code edits.
Verification key: [V] = I read the primary paper text; [S] = search-result summary or abstract only; [B] = blog/marketing, unverified.

## 1. What rigorous tests say about SMC/ICT components

**Bottom line: I found no credible, reproducible, cost-inclusive public test showing that order blocks, FVGs, liquidity sweeps, killzones or BOS/CHoCH have edge. Our BT1-BT7 result (gross R about 0, negative after costs) is the same as the little rigorous public evidence.**

**Documented positive expectancy after costs for SMC components, with exact conditions:** none that I could verify. The nearest conditional cases are not SMC:
- Osler round-number stop cascades: hours horizon, 1990s data, continuation not reversal. Gross only; no cost test.
- London-breakout blogs: "range-width filter" variants claimed positive on EURUSD/GBPUSD/EURGBP, but unverifiable ([B], e.g. [QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/london-breakout-strategy/), page blocked from me). Independent summaries say the plain version is mixed to losing.
- Zarattini/Aziz/Barbon ["Beat the Market"](https://ssrn.com/abstract=4824172) [S]: intraday momentum on **SPY** (not FX), 2007-2024, Sharpe 1.33 net of costs, driven by dynamic noise-area breakout plus trailing stop. Shows that intraday breakout/momentum edge exists in equity indices where intraday momentum is documented (Gao et al. style), not that it exists in FX.

## 2. Evidence for short-horizon edge in FX/gold, and how big it is relative to retail cost

The academic pattern: **real, statistically robust intraday FX regularities exist, they are flow/inventory driven, and they are about 0.2-1 pip per day in size, so almost all are eaten by full retail costs.** Details:

1. **Fix reversal ("W pattern")**: Krohn, Mueller & Whelan, ["FX Fixings and Returns around the Clock"](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3521370), J. Finance 2024 (I read the working-paper text [V]; direct SSRN fetch was 403 but [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13306) hosts the published version). G9 vs USD, 5-min data, 1999-2018. USD appreciates into the Tokyo (09:55 JST = 00:55 UTC), Frankfurt/ECB (14:15 Frankfurt local) and London (16:00 London) fixes and depreciates after. Dollar portfolio: +2.1 bps/day pre-Tokyo (t=12), -2.2 bps/day post; +1.7 bps pre-London (t=4.1), -1.9 bps post (t=5.5). Present every weekday, month and year. Stronger when implied vol is high and spreads are wide. Conditional version: the return in the last hour before the fix negatively predicts the next window (that is a sweep-and-reverse structure at a specific clock time). **Cost reality (authors' own Section V):** gross 11-14% a year trading EUR/GBP/JPY around fixes; with full indicative quoted spreads returns and Sharpe turn negative; with spreads cut 50%, +3.4% to +6.6% a year, Sharpe 0.5-0.7 for the best case (EUR around London fix, about 0.65). 2 bps on EURUSD is about 0.2 pip, so a per-trade edge of order 0.2-0.5 pip against a round trip cost we measured at about 1 pip. Fit only as a timing tilt or filter, or if Exness Zero's real cost is truly below the paper's 50%-spread case.
2. **Time-of-day depreciation**: Breedon & Ranaldo, ["Intraday patterns in FX returns and order flow"](https://www.snb.ch/public/asset/en/www-snb-ch/publications/research/working-papers/2011/working_paper_2011_04/publications0_en/working_paper_2011_04.n.pdf), JMCB 2013 [V]. Six pairs, hourly EBS data 1997-2007. A currency tends to depreciate during its own local trading hours (market participants are net buyers of their home currency's counterpart in their own hours; visible in order flow). Net of EBS bid/ask, "most of these simple time-of-day strategies are not profitable when costs are included" except **EUR/USD** (Sharpe about 1.3 short-morning, 0.9 long-afternoon), 1997-2007 data on institutional spreads. Decay since 2007 is expected and untested by them.
3. **Month-end hedging flows at the London 4 pm fix**: Melvin & Prins, ["Equity hedging and exchange rates at the London 4 p.m. fix"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418114000779) [S]. Passive hedgers rebalance at the fix; equity gains over the month predict currency depreciation into the month-end fix, followed by a reversal. About 12 trades a year, so sample is inherently tiny. I could not verify magnitudes.
4. **Trend/time-series momentum**: Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, ["Time series momentum"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X11002613) [S], 58 futures including currencies, 1-12 month lookbacks. Menkhoff et al., ["Currency momentum strategies"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X12001353) [S], cross-sectional spread up to 10% a year, but "sensitive to transaction costs" and concentrated in high-cost currencies. A recent walk-forward study of 7 majors, daily bars, 2003-2025 with swap and fixed transaction costs ([QuantInsti/EPAT](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/trend-following-strategies-major-currency-markets-epat-project/) [B-quant, student project]): only USDJPY (TSMOM Sharpe 0.78) and EURUSD (0.54) cleared Sharpe 0.5; 7-pair portfolio 0.43. Higher leverage destroyed value through financing. So low-frequency trend is the one documented source with net-positive evidence, but small (Sharpe 0.4-0.8) and requires years to verify.
5. **Announcement news**: Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold & Vega, ["Micro effects of macro announcements"](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2F000282803321455151) [S]: surprises cause conditional mean jumps, adjustment is fast and volatility stays elevated. Speed of the jump means retail cannot capture the first move; continuation after the jump is not established in the sources I found. Robot Wealth's [NFP drift](https://robotwealth.com/exploiting-the-non-farm-payrolls-drift/) [B, self-declared data-snooped, no costs] notes EURUSD drifts in the eventual direction before NFP (2009-2018), i.e. not tradeable ex ante.
6. **Intraday momentum**: Elaut, Frömmel & Lampaert, ["Intraday momentum in FX"](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2694985) [S]: first half-hour return predicts last half-hour, but in **RUB/USD** on MICEX, driven by liquidity providers' overnight risk aversion. Not shown for major pairs or gold. Equity index intraday momentum (SPY) is the well-documented case.
7. **Gold**: [EVTradeLabs XAUUSD seasonality](https://evtradelabs.com/blog/xauusd-seasonality-day-month-session) [B, but transparent] on 23 years of M1: Asia (00-07 UTC) drift +0.029% (p<0.001), weekend gap +0.034%, Friday +0.104% (p=0.002), January +3.4% (p=0.005); London and NY sessions not significant. Author's own caveats: about 60 tests with 5 hits and no multiple-testing correction, 2020-24 bull market inflation, typical gold cost 0.05-0.15% per trade "enough to eat the Asian drift". The Asia drift (0.029%) is smaller than the 0.037% median spread they measured. Treat as beta from a bull market, not edge.
8. **Overnight equity-index drift** ([Boyarchenko, Larsen, Whelan](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr917), [update: disappearing](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)) [S]: 2-3 am ET window, about 3.6% a year 1998-2019, close to zero since 2021. It is a good example of how a published flow anomaly decays.
9. **Carry**: documented premium, but pays off over months with crash risk (2024 yen unwind); not a short-horizon edge. Retail swap includes broker markup; requires swap data from Exness we do not have in the harness. 2021-26 also contains one big carry regime (USD/JPY), so single-regime risk.

**How SMC concepts could be reframed to align with real flows** (this is my synthesis, not an established result):
- Replace "swing-high liquidity" with **round-number levels (xx00/xx50) and prior-day extremes when they coincide with the clock of a known flow** (fixes, month-end, London open inventory rebalancing). Test direction both ways because Osler predicts **continuation after stop-cluster breach** and **reversal at take-profit clusters** (approach from the other side).
- Use a sweep as a **conditioning variable for the fix reversal**: a run-up into a fix that also sweeps the session extreme is the extreme case of Krohn's "last-hour return negatively predicts next window".
- Use SMC time windows purely as **cost/activity gates** (BT7 already showed this), not as signal.

## 3. Why our SMC rules show gross about 0 (diagnosis from BT1-BT7)

1. **Symmetric brackets on a near-martingale give gross zero by construction.** BT1: sweep reversal at 1R wins 50.0% exactly; at 2R wins 33-35% (= 1/3 break-even). Any entry pattern that is a deterministic function of past prices, combined with a fixed stop/target, has expected gross R = (conditional drift) x (holding time) / risk. If the pattern carries no conditional drift, R gross is zero at every TP:SL ratio and in every session (BT1 TP 1/2/3R identical, BT7 mirror control also negative). The only ways to a gross edge are (a) a conditioning variable that predicts drift, or (b) a payoff shape that exploits vol clustering. Our tests flip entry/stop/target and get the same zero, which is what the theory predicts.
2. **The "liquidity" being swept is not observable.** FX is OTC with no central book; the only measured stop clusters (Osler) are at round numbers and are used for continuation. Swing highs/lows are visible to everyone and no test finds them special: BT1 H1/H4 swings, prior-day H/L, Asian range, equal highs/lows all about -0.14 to -0.22R, and BT7: prior-day H/L sweeps reverse only 11-19%.
3. **Costs are large relative to the small stops SMC needs.** Median stop 7 pips (M15) or 13 pips (H1); commission + spread = 0.05-0.21R per trade (BT1), 0.069R M15 to 0.014R H4 (BT4). Every result improves monotonically with timeframe only because cost per R shrinks (BT2: M15 -0.19, H4 -0.02; BT3 M5 -0.26 to H1 -0.06; BT4 M15 -0.13 R). Gross stays at -0.03 to -0.06 R on every TF.
4. **Selection bias on limit entries.** BT1 retrace entry -0.27R, BT4 OB entry -0.15R with only 30-45% fill, BT2 distal -0.67R: only trades that go against you come back to fill (winners never fill). Chase entries are better than retrace entries, opposite to the SMC dogma.
5. **Structure labels are lagging and mostly noise.** BT4: 59% of "BOS" events are minor breaks; raw move after break-bar close is slightly **negative** on M15/M30 (-0.02 to -0.13 ATR, t up to -6, mean reversion) and about zero on H1/H4. BT5: H1 structure trend is worse than random at 24h/3d (z=-4.4 / -9.7) because it lags and over-persists.
6. **The few real signals in our own results are not SMC signals and are small vs cost:** (a) M15 mean reversion after breaks, 0.02-0.13 ATR versus 0.11-0.20 ATR round trip; (b) H4 structure trend and price vs EMA200(H1) drift +1.11 / +0.38 pips after cost over 3 days (z=7.5 / 4.9, but overlapping windows overstate z); (c) BT5 context: fresh H4 breakout or its retest is the worst context (-3 to -5 pips/24h), trend regime (ADX 25+) is worse than range, discount-half buys / premium-half sells beat the opposite; (d) BT3 London fills gross about +0.04-0.07R with a liquidity target, n small; (e) BT7 cost gating (London/NY hours, skip 20-24 UTC and EURGBP).
7. **Multiplicity in our own work is large.** BT4 ran 96 DEV cells; 4 were positive, best +0.058R, the top 3 went to VAL and 2 flipped sign to -0.11/-0.13R. BT3/BT2 per-pair "winners" flipped between DEV and VAL. This is what luck gives, so pair-level or hour-level winners from BT1-BT4 must not seed the next round.
8. **Reward-to-risk mismatch in BT5.** Win rate 17-20% vs planned RR 3-20 because HTF liquidity targets are far; most trades stop out in 2.7-4.4 h. Fixed targets far away do not create edge; they change variance.

## 4. Ranked hypotheses for BT6 / BT9+

Ranking is by my prior probability that the test shows **a gross edge that survives measured cost**, not by how much SMC flavour it has. Design principle for all: **first an event study in pips (forward return conditional on signal minus unconditional return of the same instrument/hours), only then a bracket/exit. Go/no-go: gross pips per trade at least 2x the measured round-trip cost in pips, same sign in at least 8 of 11 instruments and in each calendar year 2021-2026, block bootstrap or Newey-West t above 3 (overlap-corrected).** Run each on DEV only, freeze, then a single pass on VAL, TEST last and once. Count every variant you look at, apply Benjamini-Hochberg across the whole BT6-BT9 family.

### H1. Multi-day trend filter as the position (HTF bias as the trade, not as a filter)
- **Rule:** daily bars, UTC 22:00 close (or H4 bars). Signal = sign of the past 63-day and 126-day return (average of the two signs, or both must agree); position = +1/-1 unit scaled to inverse 20-day ATR; rebalance only at the daily close, only if signal flips or weekly. Instruments: all 10 FX + XAUUSD, equal risk. Holding: days to weeks. Second version: price vs EMA200 on H1 (BT5's +0.38 pips/3d) held for 3 days with cost charged once. Add swap if the harness has it; otherwise report gross and net of a ±1-2 pips/day swap sensitivity.
- **Why it could work:** time-series momentum is the most replicated result (Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen); BT5 already measured a small positive 3-day drift after cost for exactly this signal (H4 structure +1.11 pips, EMA200 H1 +0.38 pips). Cost is amortised over days, so the cost/R problem disappears.
- **Sources:** [MOP 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X11002613), [Menkhoff et al. 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X12001353), [EPAT walk-forward on 7 majors](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/trend-following-strategies-major-currency-markets-epat-project/), BT5 results.
- **Caveats/overfit:** expected Sharpe only 0.4-0.8 with wide error over 5.5 years (about 5-6 independent yearly observations). Gold's 2021-26 bull run makes a long-biased gold result meaningless; require the short side to contribute or report the long/short split separately. Do not tune the lookback; use 63/126 as given.

### H2. Intraday extension fade in the lowest-cost windows (regime: range, not trend)
- **Rule:** EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, XAUUSD only, 12:00-16:00 UTC winter and 12:00-15:00 UTC summer (overlap; cost about 2-5% of the hourly move per BT7). Event: M15 close beyond the prior 12-bar (3h) high or low by 0.5 ATR(14) (an "extension" or BOS/sweep with displacement). Trade: fade at the next M15 open, market order. Exit: time exit after 4 M15 bars (1h) and 8 bars, plus stop at 1.5 ATR. Condition on H4 ADX below 25 (BT5: range regime better than trend) and on "not a fresh H4 breakout or its retest" (BT5 worst context, -3 to -5 pips/24h). Both directions symmetric.
- **Why it could work:** own data: BT4 raw study found slight mean reversion after M15/M30 break closes (-0.02 to -0.13 ATR, t up to -6); BT5 found fresh breakouts are the worst forward context; Krohn et al. find conditional reversals of last-hour returns and stronger reversals in high-vol/wide-spread states (inventory risk); Osler take-profit clusters produce reversals. So there is a real, significant, small reversal; the only question is whether it beats cost when cost is confined to the cheapest hours.
- **Sources:** [Krohn, Mueller, Whelan 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13306), [Osler 2003/05](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf), BT4 section 2 and BT5 context tables.
- **Caveats/overfit:** BT4's own numbers say the reversal is below round-trip cost on M15 all-hours, so a failure here is likely. The ADX and breakout-context conditions came from BT5 DEV, so they are already a look at the data; freeze them and test on VAL only. The 0.5 ATR and 4 bars grid: fix them now, do not search.

### H3. Fix-window reversal used as the sweep context (clock-time SMC)
- **Rule:** DST-aware fix times: Tokyo 00:55 UTC, ECB/Frankfurt 14:15 CET/CEST-local (13:15 UTC winter, 12:15 UTC summer), London 16:00 local (16:00 UTC winter, 15:00 UTC summer). Measure USD-leg return over the 60 minutes before each fix (for XXXUSD pairs sign flipped so "USD up"). If USD moved up by at least 1 ATR(M15) in that hour, and the last bar before the fix also swept the session or prior-day high vs USD, go **short USD (long foreign)** at the fix, hold 2 hours. Mirror for USD down. Do the London fix on EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY first (highest liquidity), Tokyo on USDJPY/AUDUSD/NZDUSD.
- **Why it could work:** the W pattern is documented over 20 years, every year, with the conditional form (last-hour return negatively predicts next window) being exactly a sweep-then-reverse at a fixed clock time; strongest in high-vol and wide-spread states.
- **Source:** [Krohn, Mueller, Whelan (JF 2024)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13306); size and costs verified from the paper text (2 bps per window; positive net only with spreads halved; Sharpe 0.5-0.7 best case).
- **Caveats/overfit:** unconditional pattern is about 0.2 pip on EURUSD, so it will fail unless conditioning concentrates the effect (their result on high-vol days) and Exness Zero costs are below their assumption. Requires clean DST handling (BT7 already has it). 1999-2018 sample; anomalies decay (see overnight drift). Do not scan other clock times looking for a "fix-like" effect.

### H4. Round-number stop cascades and take-profit bounces (Osler reframe of "sweep")
- **Rule:** levels = every xx00 and xx50 on FX (in the pip convention Osler used) and gold $10/$50 multiples (declared in advance). Event study on M5 bars, two arms: (A) **breach**: price closes an M5 bar 3+ pips (gold $1) beyond a level; measure the next 1, 2, 4 hours in the breach direction; (B) **approach**: price touches within 2 pips of the level from one side without breaching; measure the bounce. Compare against the same measures at random "placebo" levels (xx23, xx77). If arm A shows continuation net of cost, trade a stop-entry at level + buffer with time exit at 2 h.
- **Why it could work:** only order-book-based evidence in the literature; trends after breaching are unusually rapid, response lasts hours; take-profit response is reversal.
- **Sources:** [Osler stop-loss cascades](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf) [V], [Osler support/resistance](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2000/rp000622a) [S].
- **Caveats/overfit:** 1990s data; retail round-number awareness and algos may have arbitraged it; effects were significant over hours, not days. Use placebo levels as the null; a positive vs zero test is not enough. This overlaps BT1 "breakout -0.23R" (which used swing levels, not round numbers), so a failure here mostly closes the "liquidity" question.

### H5. BT5's premium/discount signal as a 24h mean-reversion tilt inside range regimes
- **Rule:** H4 range = last 30 H4 bars high/low; regime = ADX(14, H4) below 25 and no H4 close outside the range in the last 6 bars. Daily at 22:00 UTC: if price in lowest 25% of the range, long; highest 25%, short; hold 24h, market exit, stop 2 ATR(D1) beyond the range edge. 11 instruments.
- **Why it could work:** BT5 reported the discount/premium split as "measurably better" than the opposite and range regime > trend regime, both consistent with FX mean reversion at 1-day horizon and with BT4 M15 reversion.
- **Sources:** BT5 report (own data), [Krohn et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13306) for inventory-driven reversal, [Menkhoff](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X12001353) for the horizon contrast.
- **Caveats/overfit:** originates from our DEV data (post-hoc), no magnitude reported; 30 bars/25% are free parameters, fix them now. It is the opposite bet to H1 (momentum at weeks, reversion at a day) so run both and check they do not just offset; correlation of the two return streams is itself a test.

### H6. Month-end London-fix hedge flow
- **Rule:** last business day of each month; compute MSCI World (or S&P 500 proxy) month-to-date return from an external daily series; if positive by more than 2%, short the foreign currency vs USD (short EURUSD, GBPUSD, AUDUSD; long USDJPY) from 12:00 to 15:55 London time, exit at the fix; if negative by more than 2%, mirror. Optional second leg: reverse after the fix (their reported reversal).
- **Why it could work:** passive equity hedgers rebalance FX exposures at the 4 pm fix once a month; direction determined by equity performance.
- **Source:** [Melvin & Prins](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418114000779) [S]. Magnitude not verified.
- **Caveats/overfit:** about 66 events in 5.5 years (about 1 t-stat unit per year at best), high probability of false positive and false negative; direction sign convention must be fixed from the paper before looking; needs external equity data. Report as a diary of 66 numbers rather than a strategy. Prior low.

### H7. Scheduled-news continuation after the first burst
- **Rule:** tier-1 releases only (NFP, CPI, FOMC, ECB, BoE, plus XAUUSD on US CPI/NFP), calendar fixed in advance from a public list. At t+5 minutes: if the 5-minute move exceeded 2x its median 5-min range, and spread has returned to under 2x normal, enter in the direction of the move at the t+5 close with market order and exit at t+60 or 1.5 ATR stop. Compare with fade.
- **Why it could work:** jumps at announcements are documented; volatility elevated for hours; some evidence of continued drift 2-3 h after NFP among practitioners.
- **Sources:** [Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold, Vega (AER 2003)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2F000282803321455151) [S], [Robot Wealth NFP drift](https://robotwealth.com/exploiting-the-non-farm-payrolls-drift/) [B; explicitly data-snooped].
- **Caveats/overfit:** M1 backtests badly understate slippage and spread widening at news; our fills at M1 bar prices are unrealistic there. Few events (about 6 x 12 x 5.5 = 400 per pair at most across all types, but only 1-2 clean types). A positive here should be treated as unverified until a demo forward test with real fills.

### H8 (lowest, flagged as overfit-prone). Gold calendar/session drift and carry tilt
- **Rule (gold):** long XAUUSD from 00:00 to 07:00 UTC Mon-Fri (Asia drift) or Friday close-to-close; **Rule (carry):** each month rank the 10 FX pairs by policy-rate differential, hold top 3 long carry, bottom 3 short, with swap included.
- **Why it could work:** [EVTradeLabs](https://evtradelabs.com/blog/xauusd-seasonality-day-month-session) reports p<0.001 for Asia drift on 23 years of M1; carry is a documented premium.
- **Caveats/overfit:** gold drift (0.029% per session) is below the median spread (0.037%) the source itself measured, comes from about 60 uncorrected tests and a bull market, and 2021-26 is a gold bull run; any long gold result is beta. Carry needs swap data, has crash risk (2024 yen), and one regime in our sample. Include only as a **negative control** or if a dedicated out-of-regime check is possible.

## 5. Overfitting checklist for the next round

- **Do not promote pair-level or hour-level winners.** BT2, BT3, BT4 winners all flipped sign between DEV and VAL. Pooled results only, with per-instrument sign consistency as a check, not a selector.
- **Count trials.** Across BT1-BT7 we have run hundreds of cells; add the new hypotheses to that count and apply BH/FDR. Report the number of variants looked at next to every "best" number.
- **Freeze parameters before the run, from the source paper** (lookbacks 63/126, fix times, 1 ATR, 4 bars). No grids on entry parameters; at most 2 exits per hypothesis.
- **Overlapping-window t-stats are inflated** (BT5 z=7.5/9.7 uses overlapping 3-day windows on H1 bars). Use non-overlapping samples or Newey-West / block bootstrap.
- **One regime.** 2021-26 = USD rally, hiking cycle, gold bull, yen carry unwind. Require per-year consistency and check long/short split.
- **Gross before net.** Judge each hypothesis on gross pips first, then on measured cost (spread from data + commission). If gross < cost even in the cheapest window (London/NY overlap, EURUSD/USDJPY), stop; do not search for a "better" filter.
- **Execution realism.** Anything around news, fixes and rollover needs live spread. Bar spreads in M1 history are averages; check a worst-case spread stress (2x) on the fix and news trades.
- **Keep TEST untouched** until a single pre-declared candidate is chosen.
- **Hindsight sensitivity:** SMC labels (OB, sweeps, BOS) look great on charts because the label is chosen after the outcome; the codified versions in our harness use causal confirmation and that is why they show zero. Do not "fix" that by loosening causality.
